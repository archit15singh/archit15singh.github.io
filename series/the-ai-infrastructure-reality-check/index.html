Where will the next AI infrastructure bottleneck move?

Part of The AI Infrastructure Reality Check. If I had to find the next bottleneck in an AI buildout, I would look for what sits idle. Accelerators in storage point to a site readiness problem. A running cluster with a saturated network points somewhere else. Spare capacity after customers have been onboarded puts the question to sales and product. All three situations can follow a successful hardware purchase. There is evidence that several limits coexist. In March 2025, Micron said HBM3E requires roughly three times the silicon of DDR5 per bit. The IEA estimates electricity use at AI-focused data centers rose 50% in 2025. At the level of an inference service, the DistServe authors found that moving prefill and decode to separate GPU pools reduced interference in their tests, with placement decisions now depending on network bandwidth for the KV-cache transfer. Each observation has a different scope. Together they explain why improving supply in one layer can bring a different constraint into view. What happens when a GPU cluster hits a power limit?

Part of The AI Infrastructure Reality Check. A cluster with 98% service-slot utilization missed nearly half its deadlines in a toy power-curtailment experiment. Under the same site power ceiling, a cluster that kept more rack groups active at lower power got every request in on time. The result reverses when capping has a bigger effect on performance. Before choosing a policy, measure the curve. There is real work behind the premise. The Zeus authors evaluated power caps and batch-size choices across several deep-learning training workloads and reported energy-efficiency gains ranging from 15.3% to 75.8% in their experiments. Google reported signing 1 GW of demand-response capacity across its U.S. data-center agreements in March 2026 and says it can shift a portion of its machine-learning work. The research measures specific training jobs. The Google figure describes contracted flexibility. Neither supplies a latency curve for an inference fleet.
