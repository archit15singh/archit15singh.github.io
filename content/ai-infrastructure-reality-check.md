Follow an AI purchase from memory wafers through powered racks to the bill for a customer request. The series uses public filings, published surveys and benchmarks, and reproducible scenarios to explain how those parts of the buildout behave.

## Start here

- [How AI demand puts upward pressure on DDR5 prices](/posts/2026-11-20-how-ai-demand-raises-ddr5-prices/) Follow two routes from AI infrastructure orders to PC memory costs, then see where the evidence stops.
- [There isn't one AI bubble. There are six different businesses.](/posts/2026-11-20-six-different-businesses/) Follow the invoices from DRAM fabs to paying AI users to see who earns revenue and who carries the risk.
- [The GPU break-even equation nobody shows you](/posts/2026-11-20-gpu-break-even/) A per-GPU model for the price and billable hours needed to recover hardware, power, operations and the cost of capital.
- [The AI buildout is a critical-path problem](/posts/2026-11-20-ai-buildout-critical-path/) A deployment model shows why getting GPUs four months early can buy zero months of revenue and how to find the milestone that matters.
- [Five predictions that could falsify my AI boom thesis](/posts/2026-11-20-five-falsifiable-predictions/) Five public indicators with deadlines, baseline values and rules for scoring a miss.

## Chips and memory

- [What a sold-out HBM order book tells you](/posts/2026-11-20-sold-out-hbm-contracts/) Follow a memory contract through deposits, shipment, deployment, and the economics of the application it ultimately serves.
- [What the AI buildout changes for a PC builder](/posts/2026-11-20-how-ai-infrastructure-affects-pc-builds/) A matched DDR5 kit shows why posted price and stock both matter, especially for a local AI workstation.
- [Why GPU rental and RAM prices can move in opposite directions](/posts/2026-11-20-why-gpu-rent-and-ram-prices-diverge/) An AWS rate cut and a rising PC DRAM contract market show how installed capacity and fresh manufacturing respond to different constraints.

## Physical infrastructure

- [Where will the next AI infrastructure bottleneck move?](/posts/2026-11-20-next-ai-infrastructure-bottleneck/) Track how scarcity can move from accelerator supply to power, networking, and customer demand using the throughput of one workload.
- [What happens when a GPU cluster hits a power limit?](/posts/2026-11-20-gpu-cluster-power-limit/) Under the same site power cap, eight fast rack groups and twelve slower ones produce different queues. The better policy depends on the workload.

## Capital and unit economics

- [Will GPUs become obsolete before they pay for themselves?](/posts/2026-11-20-gpu-economic-life/) A reproducible three- versus five-year cash-flow model shows why a working GPU can fall short of earning back its purchase price.
- [AI capex is climbing. What is happening to cash flow?](/posts/2026-11-20-ai-capex-cash-flow/) A filing-based comparison of Microsoft, Alphabet and Meta measures how much cash remained after equipment spending in the September quarters of 2025 and 2026.
- [The circular money flows behind AI infrastructure](/posts/2026-11-20-circular-ai-financing/) Anthropic's AMD disclosure and CoreWeave's NVIDIA agreements show how hardware supply, equity and capacity commitments can point in opposite directions.
- [The true cost of serving one million AI tokens](/posts/2026-11-20-true-cost-million-ai-tokens/) Three public DeepSeek-R1 benchmark runs on the same rack show why a token cost estimate needs a latency target and an occupancy assumption.

## Demand and the bullish case

- [AI demand, GPU utilization and profit are three different metrics](/posts/2026-11-20-ai-demand-utilization-profit/) CoreWeave's Q3 2026 filing and a four-stage operating funnel show what each number can actually tell you.
- [The strongest evidence against the AI bubble narrative](/posts/2026-11-20-strongest-bullish-countercase/) Paid usage, measured business adoption and infrastructure consumption make the bullish case, with different limits.
