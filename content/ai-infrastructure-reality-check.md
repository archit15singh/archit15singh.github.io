Five articles follow an AI purchase from memory wafers to customer revenue. The series draws on public filings, price observations and published benchmarks, with calculators and datasets for readers who want to check the numbers.

## Read the series

1. [How the AI buildout reaches your DDR5 bill](/posts/2026-11-20-how-ai-demand-raises-ddr5-prices/) Trace HBM and server demand through wafer allocation, supply contracts, retail listings and GPU rental prices.
2. [GPU economics from break-even to cost per token](/posts/2026-11-20-gpu-break-even/) Use a break-even calculator, a hardware-life model and three MLPerf runs to price the work a GPU can deliver.
3. [What limits an AI cluster from delivery to peak load](/posts/2026-11-20-ai-buildout-critical-path/) Model first billable capacity, moving bottlenecks and how a power cap changes inference queues.
4. [AI infrastructure cash flow and who finances the buildout](/posts/2026-11-20-ai-capex-cash-flow/) Compare three hyperscalers’ equipment spending, then follow the commitments, utilization and financing of a GPU operator.
5. [Six AI businesses and five tests of the boom](/posts/2026-11-20-six-different-businesses/) Follow the money across six businesses, examine the strongest demand evidence and revisit five predictions in 2027.
