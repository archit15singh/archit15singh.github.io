The AI Infrastructure Reality Check

Sixteen articles on the economics and engineering of the AI buildout, with source data, models and calculators.

Follow an AI purchase from memory wafers through powered racks to the bill for a customer request. The series uses public filings, published surveys and benchmarks, and reproducible scenarios to explain how those parts of the buildout behave.

Start here#

  • How AI demand puts upward pressure on DDR5 prices Follow two routes from AI infrastructure orders to PC memory costs, then see where the evidence stops.
  • There isn’t one AI bubble. There are six different businesses. Follow the invoices from DRAM fabs to paying AI users to see who earns revenue and who carries the risk.
  • The GPU break-even equation nobody shows you A per-GPU model for the price and billable hours needed to recover hardware, power, operations and the cost of capital.
  • The AI buildout is a critical-path problem A deployment model shows why getting GPUs four months early can buy zero months of revenue and how to find the milestone that matters.
  • Five predictions that could falsify my AI boom thesis Five public indicators with deadlines, baseline values and rules for scoring a miss.

  • What a sold-out HBM order book tells you Follow a memory contract through deposits, shipment, deployment, and the economics of the application it ultimately serves.
  • What the AI buildout changes for a PC builder A matched DDR5 kit shows why posted price and stock both matter, especially for a local AI workstation.
  • Why GPU rental and RAM prices can move in opposite directions An AWS rate cut and a rising PC DRAM contract market show how installed capacity and fresh manufacturing respond to different constraints.

Physical infrastructure#

  • Where will the next AI infrastructure bottleneck move? Track how scarcity can move from accelerator supply to power, networking, and customer demand using the throughput of one workload.
  • What happens when a GPU cluster hits a power limit? Under the same site power cap, eight fast rack groups and twelve slower ones produce different queues. The better policy depends on the workload.

Capital and unit economics#

  • Will GPUs become obsolete before they pay for themselves? A reproducible three- versus five-year cash-flow model shows why a working GPU can fall short of earning back its purchase price.
  • AI capex is climbing. What is happening to cash flow? A filing-based comparison of Microsoft, Alphabet and Meta measures how much cash remained after equipment spending in the September quarters of 2025 and 2026.
  • The circular money flows behind AI infrastructure Anthropic’s AMD disclosure and CoreWeave’s NVIDIA agreements show how hardware supply, equity and capacity commitments can point in opposite directions.
  • The true cost of serving one million AI tokens Three public DeepSeek-R1 benchmark runs on the same rack show why a token cost estimate needs a latency target and an occupancy assumption.

  • AI demand, GPU utilization and profit are three different metrics CoreWeave’s Q3 2026 filing and a four-stage operating funnel show what each number can actually tell you.
  • The strongest evidence against the AI bubble narrative Paid usage, measured business adoption and infrastructure consumption make the bullish case, with different limits.
